a weighted wheel is coupled to a spiral hairspring that winds as it turns one way, unwinds the other, always pulling it back to centre: a restoring torque τ = −κ·θ proportional to how far it has turned. so the wheel oscillates — θ(t) = A·cos(2πt/T) — swinging back and forth at a period set only by its inertia I and the spring stiffness κ: T = 2π√(I/κ). the swing size A does not enter T — wide or narrow, each swing takes the same time. that is isochronism, and it is why the balance keeps time. wind the spring stiffer or lighten the wheel and it beats faster.the balance wheel + hairspring · Huygens/Hooke ~1675 · T = 2π√(I/κ), independent of amplitude (isochronism) · field: horology / harmonic oscillator
