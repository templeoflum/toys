central-limit

draws N
 
pick a source shape — flat, skewed, or two spikes, none of them a bell. now draw N values from it and keep their average; that one average is a single sample. do this many times and stack the sample-means into a histogram. at N=1 the histogram is just the source's own lopsided shape. as N grows the pile narrows and rounds into a bell — the normal curve — no matter which source you chose. the centre sits at the source mean μ; the spread is σ/√N, so it tightens like 1/√N: to halve the width you need four times the draws. that averaging-goes-bell is the whole idea.the central limit theorem · mean of N i.i.d. draws → Normal(μ, σ²/N) · spread ∝ 1/√N · field: probability
