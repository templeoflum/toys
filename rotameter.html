fluid flows up a tube that widens toward the top, past a float that would otherwise sink. the flow squeezes through the ring-shaped gap around the float and drags it upward; the float rises until that gap is wide enough to drop the flow speed to where the drag exactly balances the float's net (submerged) weight. net weight is constant, so the balance fixes the gap area — and in a linearly-tapered tube the gap area is proportional to height, so the float settles at a height ∝ flow. read Q off the scale at the float's top edge. a heavier float, or a lighter fluid, needs more flow to reach the same mark.the rotameter (variable-area flow meter) · Karl Küppers 1908 · drag balances net weight, height reads flow · field: fluid mechanics / flow measurement
