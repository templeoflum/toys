six things the floor says — a memo lands, a word is asked, a signature is asked, a sign lands, a fix confirms, the alarm — each sounded two ways. the top row is blattner's earcons (1989): short musical motives that combine, transform, and inherit — rhythm first, register and loudness carry urgency; learn the family once and new members read free, like words. the bottom row is gaver's auditory icons (1986): caricatures of everyday events — a slide, a knock, a scratch, a stamp — meaning heard the way the world is heard, almost for free, though abstract states strain the metaphor. drag urgency to transform the whole family; drag weight to feel the icons' physics. this palette is the dispatch's notification design happening mock-first — what sounds right here gets signed, then wired. concurrent sounds obey the masking toy's law: what shares a band, hides.
