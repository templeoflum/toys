spring scale

load L
stiffness k
 
 
a coil spring hangs from a fixed hook; a load pulls its free end down. by Hooke's law the spring's stretch is proportional to the force: F = k·x, so a load L stretches it by x = L / k. a pointer fixed to the free end travels down a graduated scale exactly in step with the stretch — where it lands IS the weight. a stiffer spring (bigger k) stretches less for the same load. past the spring's elastic limit it over-stretches and no longer returns Hooke's law — the reading goes non-linear and invalid.the spring (Hooke's-law) scale · Robert Hooke 1678 · ut tensio, sic vis — x = F/k, extension proportional to load · field: elasticity / statics
