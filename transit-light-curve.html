a planet drifts across the face of its star, and while it hides part of that bright disc the total light dips. the depth of the dip is just the fraction of the star it covers — at mid-transit, (Rp/Rs)². a central pass (b=0) carves a flat-bottomed box; a grazing pass (b near 1) only clips the edge, a shallow V with no flat floor. the curve IS the reading — how Kepler and TESS weigh worlds they never see.transit photometry (exoplanet detection) · dip depth = (Rp/Rs)² · Charbonneau et al. 2000 (HD 209458b), the Kepler/TESS method
