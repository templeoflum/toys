the roundabout

input
gap
 
cars circulate a ring with no signal and no controller — each entrant decides for itself. an approaching car yields: it waits at the entry until it sees a gap in the circulating stream wide enough to accept, then merges. circulating traffic has priority; the entrant commits only on a gap it judges safe, so no car ever enters into an occupied slot. raise the gap threshold and entrants grow cautious — safer, but queues build and throughput falls; lower it and they merge aggressively — higher throughput, tighter margins. smooth continuous coordination, each participant yielding by its own local rule.the roundabout · yield-based decentralized coordination · gap acceptance vs. throughput
